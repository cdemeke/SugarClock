Before you start

SugarClock (Sugar Clock) turns a Ulanzi TC001 into a Wi-Fi blood sugar display. It reads Dexcom Share over the internet; it does not pair directly with a sensor over Bluetooth.

  • A Ulanzi TC001 with SugarClock installed, a USB-C data cable for installation, and 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi.
  • A working Dexcom app and account that upload current readings to Dexcom Share. Check sharing in the official app before configuring the clock.
  • The username and password for the account that supplies the readings. A follower's login or a Clarity clinic sharing code is not the clock's Dexcom login.

The firmware offers US (share2.dexcom.com) and International (shareous1.dexcom.com). These are account-server choices, not the clock's physical location. It has no separate China or Japan server option. Check your account's service before assuming compatibility with a regional Dexcom product.

Connect your Dexcom account

  1. Install SugarClock and connect it to Wi-Fi. Open the clock's local settings page using the IP address it displays. Your browser needs access to the same local network.
  2. Enable Share in your Dexcom app. For G7, Dexcom documents Connections → Share. Complete the sharing prompts and follower setup in the official apps. Menu names differ across Dexcom products.
  3. In the clock's Settings → Blood Sugar tab, set Source Type to Dexcom Share.
  4. Enter the uploading account's Username and Password, then choose the matching Region.
  5. Save the settings and use Test Connection. Start with the default 60-second polling interval; faster polling cannot make the upstream CGM produce a new reading.
  6. Compare the clock's value, trend, and reading age with the official Dexcom app. Choose the display units you use and check them again after saving.

Keep the source app and its internet connection working. The clock also needs internet access to reach Dexcom Share; a successful Wi-Fi connection alone does not prove that sharing is working.

If the clock has no new reading

Login fails
Check the uploading account's credentials and region. Confirm you can sign in to the official Dexcom service. Do not use the follower's credentials.
Null session or an empty result
Confirm Share is enabled and working in the Dexcom app. Complete any follower invitation or account action, and check that the app has a current reading. The firmware requests readings from the last ten minutes.
Wi-Fi connects but Test Connection fails
Check DNS and outbound internet access to the configured Dexcom server on TCP 443. Guest-network sign-in portals can prevent the clock from reaching it.
The last number stays visible
Check reading age as well as the value. Delayed or missing upstream readings can leave the previous number on screen. Its stale color is a cue to check the source app, not evidence of a new reading.

Your connection and settings

Configure account credentials only on your clock's local page, not on this website. Use a trusted local network and do not expose the settings page to the internet. See the installation reporting notice before installing.

Current firmware limitation: the Dexcom and Custom URL clients do not validate the remote TLS certificate. An HTTPS address alone does not remove that limitation. Keep this in mind when deciding where to use these integrations.

Not a medical device

SugarClock is an unofficial DIY project and is not affiliated with or endorsed by Dexcom, Abbott, Nightscout, or Ulanzi. Readings can be delayed or missing, and the clock may not sound alerts when its data is stale. Do not use SugarClock for treatment decisions or as a replacement for your CGM's own alarms; always confirm with your CGM receiver, app, or blood glucose meter.
